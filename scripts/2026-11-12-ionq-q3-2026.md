Welcome to this earnings brief for IonQ, ticker I-O-N-Q. This episode covers the company's third-quarter results for twenty twenty-six. The quarter ended on September thirtieth. IonQ released the numbers on Thursday, November fifth, twenty twenty-six, and held its conference call that afternoon. On the call were Hanley Donofrio, vice president of investor relations; Niccolo de Masi, chairman and chief executive officer; and Inder Singh, chief operating officer and chief financial officer.

This brief is based on the Motley Fool transcript of that call, the company's earnings release, and contemporaneous coverage of the results, including published consensus estimates and the stock's reaction. I will cover the results against expectations, the raised outlook, management's main points, notable analyst questions from the transcript, and what to watch next.

Here is the headline. IonQ reported record quarterly revenue of one hundred seventy-three point eight million dollars, up three hundred thirty-six percent from a year earlier. Management called it the strongest quarter in the company's history and the sixth consecutive record quarter. That figure beat published Wall Street estimates by a wide margin. Investing.com said the consensus revenue estimate was one hundred forty-one point seven one million dollars. A Zacks recap on Yahoo Finance said the result surpassed the consensus mark by about nineteen percent. On earnings, the picture depends on which measure you use. G.A.A.P. earnings were one dollar and forty-two cents a share, compared with a loss of three dollars and fifty-eight cents a year earlier, but that profit was driven by a large non-cash warrant gain. Adjusted earnings, the figure most estimates focus on, were a loss of twenty-nine cents a share. Investing.com said that beat a consensus loss of thirty-one cents. Zacks put the consensus at a loss of thirty cents. Either way, it was a modest beat on the adjusted loss, and a large beat on revenue.

The company also raised its full-year twenty twenty-six revenue guidance to five hundred five million to five hundred fifteen million dollars, from a previous range of four hundred fifty million to four hundred sixty million dollars. The company said that earlier range had been announced at its September Investor Day, according to the Zacks recap. The midpoint of the new range, five hundred ten million dollars, sits well above the analyst consensus Investing.com and Zacks both put near four hundred fifty-five million dollars. IonQ also raised its expectation for year-over-year growth in quantum computing revenue to one hundred ten percent, from a previous target of one hundred percent.

Chief Executive Officer Niccolo de Masi opened by framing the quarter as proof that IonQ is entering a new phase of growth. He said the company has built a quantum platform spanning computing, networking, sensing, and security, supported by vertically integrated manufacturing, and that the focus is now on translating those advantages into broader customer adoption, commercial scale, and long-term shareholder value. He said, quote, Our third quarter results are proof of our strategy in action, end quote. He also said third-quarter revenue of one hundred seventy-three point eight million dollars, quote, reflects increasing demand for our quantum technologies and the benefits of our vertically integrated quantum manufacturing capabilities through SkyWater, end quote, in the earnings release.

The revenue mix shows how much the story has changed. The IonQ Platform segment, which covers the company's quantum products, delivered ninety point three million dollars, up one hundred twenty-seven percent from a year earlier. De Masi called that revenue scale a record for the reporting quantum industry in its own right, and said the quarter was especially strong in quantum computing, with new contracts signed around the world for the company's sixth-generation Superion two hundred fifty-six systems. He said IonQ has sold out all of its two hundred fifty-six capacity in twenty twenty-seven.

The other half of the quarter came from SkyWater. Foundry services contributed eighty-three point five million dollars. That reflects about two months of operations after the acquisition closed at the end of July. The earnings release said revenue from non-IonQ quantum customers at SkyWater was up more than thirty percent year over year, and that the company had launched SkyWater Quantum Solutions. De Masi said SkyWater is home to what he called the industry's most advanced and diverse quantum manufacturing capabilities, and that revenue from within the quantum ecosystem grew as the company added new customers and expanded with existing ones.

Singh, on the financial review, said the results exceeded even the company's own internal modeling, driven by strength across the board, and that closing SkyWater lets IonQ offer customers a vertically integrated company, not just a platform company. Remaining performance obligations, or R.P.O., the contracted revenue not yet recognized, stood at one point two billion dollars, including six hundred eighty-nine million dollars from the IonQ Platform and four hundred seventy-nine million dollars from SkyWater. In the quarter, IonQ signed two hundred ninety million dollars of new contracts in the IonQ Platform segment, of which one hundred eighty million dollars was attributed to quantum computing systems.

Several of those awards were named. A Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency sensing contract was valued at twenty-eight million dollars for the delivery of twenty-five next-generation atomic clocks, with a thirty million dollar option for one hundred additional units. A contract with S.D.T. in South Korea was valued at thirty-three million dollars for a Superion two hundred fifty-six system and a quantum networking memory solution. Florida International University signed a twenty-five million dollar contract for a Superion two hundred fifty-six system. A deal with Congruity three sixty was valued at eight point two million dollars, focused on post-quantum cryptography and quantum key distribution. Management also said about forty percent of IonQ Platform segment revenue in the quarter came from customers buying more than one solution.

Profitability is where the beat needs a careful reading. G.A.A.P. net income attributable to IonQ was five hundred sixty-four million dollars, or one dollar and forty-two cents a share. That result was driven primarily by an eight hundred ninety-six million dollar non-cash gain from the mark-to-market valuation of warrants. Those gains can swing with the share price and with valuation assumptions. They are not cash from selling systems or foundry work. Adjusted E.B.I.T.D.A., a non-G.A.A.P. measure, was a loss of one hundred thirty-three point six million dollars. Singh said that figure reflected a heavy concentration of acquisition and integration investment in the quarter. The company recognized about forty million dollars of one-time general and administrative expenses related to the SkyWater acquisition. Operating expenses were six hundred six point nine million dollars on a G.A.A.P. basis, and three hundred six point six million dollars on a non-G.A.A.P. basis. Research and development investment was four hundred twenty-five million dollars year to date, with ninety-six point eight million dollars of non-G.A.A.P. research and development spending in the third quarter. Zacks noted that the adjusted loss of twenty-nine cents was wider than a year-ago adjusted loss of seventeen cents, even as it beat this quarter's consensus.

The balance sheet is a central part of the story. Cash, cash equivalents, and investments were one point seven billion dollars as of September thirtieth. After a two billion dollar equity offering that closed on October sixteenth, pro forma cash was three point seven billion dollars. Management has described IonQ as the best-capitalized pure-play quantum company on that measure. The offering was priced at a premium to the share price, according to the call takeaways. That capital is meant to fund the research roadmap and the integration of a larger manufacturing footprint, but it also means shareholders have already funded a substantial increase in the cash pile.

On guidance, the raise is the main official message. Full-year twenty twenty-six revenue is now expected at five hundred five million to five hundred fifteen million dollars, and quantum computing revenue is expected to grow one hundred ten percent year over year. The company did not, in the materials I used, give a formal full-year adjusted E.B.I.T.D.A. range. What Singh did say is important for the fourth quarter. He indicated that the S and P Global Capital I.Q. estimate of a negative one hundred twenty-seven million dollars of adjusted E.B.I.T.D.A. for the fourth quarter was not unreasonable. That would be a somewhat narrower loss than the third quarter's one hundred thirty-three point six million dollar adjusted E.B.I.T.D.A. deficit, but it is still a deep loss. The company is telling investors that record revenue and a guidance raise are arriving alongside heavy spending and integration costs, not instead of them.

Management's strategic points clustered around four themes.

First, Superion two hundred fifty-six demand and manufacturing. De Masi said IonQ has sold out its two hundred fifty-six capacity for twenty twenty-seven, and that new contracts for those systems were signed globally. The company also said a Superion two hundred fifty-six system will be the first on-premise quantum computer at NVIDIA's Accelerated Quantum Research Center, integrating the system with A.I. supercomputing infrastructure.

Second, a milestone with the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency, or DARPA. IonQ said it is the only trapped-ion company to advance to Stage C of DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, with a potential program value of up to three hundred million dollars. That is a selection and a potential value, not recognized revenue.

Third, the security franchise. IonQ said it is extending its quantum security platform by acquiring Elisity, a company it described as helping customers identify vulnerabilities, enforce zero trust, and provide cryptographic agility as they prepare for the quantum era. The Motley Fool summary of the call said the Elisity deal nearly doubled the company's quantum security customer base, adding about one hundred enterprise deployments to about one hundred twenty-five customers from the I.D. Quantique business. I am using that as a description of what was reported from the call, not as a separately audited customer count.

Fourth, a push toward eventual profitability, even as the current quarter's adjusted loss is large. Singh said September marked his one-year anniversary in the operating role, and that plotting a path for profitable growth is now a priority. He said some products will get there faster than others, and that the company will look across the portfolio when it allocates capital. Operationally, he pointed to monthly and quarterly business reviews, pricing strategy, deal-desk reviews, sales productivity, and merger integration. He was direct about overlap. Quote, With SkyWater and our other acquisitions, we run more back office systems and sites than we need, end quote. He said that is an example of where the company will turn its focus in twenty twenty-seven and look at its site strategy, and that it may consider tying compensation to profitability initiatives as it enters twenty twenty-seven.

Now to the question-and-answer session, using only what is on the Motley Fool transcript.

De Masi added that he had met with a number of world leaders at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting earlier that week, and that security is seen as the foundation for trusting communications, systems, and networks. Management discussed the research-center placement as a way to put a Superion two hundred fifty-six system alongside A.I. supercomputing infrastructure. Craig Ellis of B. Riley Securities congratulated management on the momentum and followed up on the comment about a busy twenty twenty-seven, noting the company's track record of strong contract signings. Singh used the question to repeat that integration and a tighter operating footprint are twenty twenty-seven work, not a claim that the fourth quarter will already look like a leaner company.

John McPeake of Rosenblatt congratulated the company on DARPA Stage C and on selling out Superion two hundred fifty-six capacity. Robert Aguanno, asking for Quinn Bolton at Needham and Company, asked two practical questions: what the SkyWater acquisition looks like now that it is closed, and what feedback and process look like in Stage C of the DARPA Quantum Benchmarking Initiative. Closing a foundry deal and advancing a government benchmarking program are not the same as converting either one into durable, high-margin revenue.

Gary Mobley of StoneX shifted the discussion away from hardware sales and asked for performance measures on cloud access or revenue from already deployed systems. His follow-up was whether selling out Superion two hundred fifty-six capacity through twenty twenty-seven creates an impediment to further sales. That is a real tension. A sold-out production slot can be a sign of demand, or a cap on near-term incremental bookings, or both. It is one of the cleaner things to watch in coming quarters: whether new signings continue, slip into later years, or shift toward other products in the portfolio.

Tyler Anderson of Craig-Hallum, asking for Richard Shannon, pressed on manufacturing capacity and on making systems that use microwave and electronic controls, then followed up on cryptographic agility and post-quantum cryptography, including the Elisity angle. The manufacturing question matters because SkyWater is supposed to be the answer to scale. The security question matters because management is asking investors to value a multi-product platform, not only computer sales.

Troy Jensen of Cantor Fitzgerald asked de Masi about the pace of future acquisitions, then asked Singh to break down the two hundred ninety million dollars of incremental IonQ Platform remaining performance obligations. Singh had already said one hundred eighty million dollars was systems. Jensen asked about the remaining one hundred ten million dollars, and whether that was primarily the sensing wins, security, or other products. Singh said the remainder came from across the rest of the portfolio, pointed to sensing and security, and said customers are starting the journey with one product and then moving to another. That is the multi-product thesis in a single answer. It is also a reminder that a large share of the new contracted value is already understood as system sales, and that the rest is a blend rather than a single second engine.

De Masi's closing remarks repeated the platform argument. He said IonQ recognized early that customers' most consequential challenges demand more than a single quantum point solution, which is why the company expanded into networking, sensing, and security. He again cited the figure that more than forty percent of the quarter's IonQ segment revenue came from customers buying more than one solution. He said that as the company scales, shared technology, manufacturing, and customer relationships should strengthen each part of the business, and that he expects IonQ, in the coming years, to approach a scale that will support structural profitability.

TipRanks also noted that the stock was still down about twenty-seven percent year to date even after Friday's jump, and that several analysts maintained Buy ratings while cutting price targets, including names that had just been on the call, such as Bank of America's Vivek Arya and B. Here is what is fair to watch next.

First, whether fourth-quarter revenue stays on a path that makes the new five hundred five to five hundred fifteen million dollar full-year range achievable, and whether the adjusted E.B.I.T.D.A. loss narrows toward the neighborhood of the one hundred twenty-seven million dollar fourth-quarter estimate Singh said was not unreasonable.

Second, SkyWater integration. The foundry contributed eighty-three point five million dollars in a partial quarter, and it also contributed a forty million dollar one-time expense and a broader overlap in systems and sites that Singh said will be a twenty twenty-seven focus. The question is how quickly the foundry becomes a durable, profitable manufacturing advantage rather than a cost and complexity story.

Third, conversion of the one point two billion dollars of remaining performance obligations, and of the two hundred ninety million dollars of new IonQ Platform contracts signed in the quarter. Sold-out Superion two hundred fifty-six capacity for twenty twenty-seven is a demand signal. It is also a delivery and timing risk.

Fourth, the mix between system sales and the rest of the platform. One hundred eighty million dollars of the new contracted value was systems. The remaining one hundred ten million dollars, and the forty percent multi-product revenue share, are the evidence for the networking, sensing, and security strategy.

Fifth, the named programs that are still more catalyst than cash: DARPA Stage C, with a potential value of up to three hundred million dollars; the NVIDIA research-center deployment; the Elisity security acquisition; and the clock, university, and networking contracts already announced.

Sixth, the path to what de Masi called structural profitability. The company is well capitalized, at three point seven billion dollars of pro forma cash after the October offering, and it is still investing heavily, with a one hundred thirty-three point six million dollar adjusted E.B.I.T.D.A. loss this quarter and hundreds of millions of dollars of research spending year to date. The next several quarters will show whether the profitability program Singh described is visible in the run rate, or still mainly a twenty twenty-seven talking point.
