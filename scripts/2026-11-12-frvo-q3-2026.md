Welcome to this earnings brief for Fervo Energy, ticker F-R-V-O. This episode covers the company's third-quarter results for twenty twenty-six. The quarter ended on September thirtieth. Fervo released the numbers on Monday, November ninth, twenty twenty-six, and held its conference call the same day. On the call were Paxton Bentzinger, senior director of corporate development and investor relations; Timothy Latimer, co-founder and chief executive officer; and David Ulrey, chief financial officer.

This brief is based on the Motley Fool transcript of that call, the company's official earnings release, and contemporaneous coverage, including published consensus estimates. I will cover the results against expectations, the updated spending outlook, management's key points, notable analyst questions, and what to watch next.

Here is the headline. This was Fervo's first quarter with commercial operations, and the financials still look like a construction company more than like a power producer. The company declared commercial operation at Cape Station Phase One, GeoBlock One, on September thirtieth, ahead of the contractual commercial operation date. The first GeoBlock is a thirty-three megawatt unit, and it contributed only a single day of commercial operation in the quarter. Revenue was one hundred sixteen thousand dollars. The earnings release also shows fifty-five thousand dollars of revenue in the year-ago quarter, and two hundred ninety thousand dollars for the first nine months of twenty twenty-six.

That revenue line slightly missed some published estimates. Seeking Alpha said G.A.A.P. earnings of a seven-cent loss beat by two cents, while revenue of twelve one-hundredths of a million dollars missed by three one-hundredths of a million dollars, which implies a consensus near one hundred fifty thousand dollars. TipRanks, citing The Fly, said the seven-cent loss was in line with a seven-cent consensus. I am not going to force those two earnings comparisons into a single official number. What is fair to say is that revenue was still tiny, as one day of commercial operation would suggest, and that the per-share result was either in line or a modest beat, depending on the estimate source, at a seven-cent net loss attributable to common stockholders.

The net income line can confuse if you stop at the first figure. Fervo reported net income of eleven point seven million dollars, but a net loss attributable to common stockholders of twenty point eight million dollars, or seven cents a share. The Motley Fool takeaway noted that the per-share loss included fourteen million dollars of non-cash stock-based compensation. Net income reflected fifteen point four million dollars of interest income and two items the company called non-recurring: an eleven point eight million dollar non-cash gain on interest-rate swaps, and a twenty-nine point nine million dollar tax benefit from tax credits earned as Cape Phase One assets were placed in service. Operating loss was forty-three point seven million dollars. General and administrative expense was thirty-nine point four million dollars, up from twenty-seven point four million dollars in the second quarter, reflecting higher stock-based compensation, public-company costs, and headcount growth. Employees numbered three hundred six at quarter-end, up from two hundred sixty-one in the second quarter, primarily in operations and construction.

In other words, the company made money on interest, a swap mark-to-market, and tax credits, while the operating business was still a sizable loss, and common stockholders still showed a loss. That is a first-power quarter, not a run-rate earnings quarter.

The spending and liquidity figures are the ones that will shape the next year. Capital expenditures were two hundred fifty-six point seven million dollars in the third quarter, compared with two hundred twenty-six point five million dollars in the second quarter, primarily at Cape Station. Fervo expects fourth-quarter capital expenditures of about four hundred fifty million to five hundred million dollars, bringing second-half twenty twenty-six spending to seven hundred million to seven hundred fifty million dollars. That is a reduction from the prior second-half range of eight hundred fifty million to nine hundred million dollars, which the company attributed to more efficient spending timelines, not to a smaller project. Unrestricted cash and cash equivalents were one point nine billion dollars, or about one billion, nine hundred sixteen million dollars on the balance sheet. Current and long-term debt totaled three hundred one point six million dollars.

On revenue guidance, the company was conservative in a specific way. It said it continues to anticipate fourth-quarter twenty twenty-six revenue in line with prior expectations, and that this will be the first full quarter of revenue from Cape Station. Revenue in twenty twenty-seven, it said, remains unchanged from prior expectations. The release did not restate the dollar amounts of those prior expectations, so I will not invent them. The official message is that the first-power milestone did not cause a change in the company's previously given revenue outlook, while the second-half capital-expenditure range came down.

Latimer's strategic message was much larger than one hundred sixteen thousand dollars of sales. In the release he said Cape Station reached first power and contractual commercial operation, and that Fervo also signed what it believes is the largest enhanced geothermal power purchase agreement to date, with Google, creating a pathway to scaling the partnership to nearly a gigawatt. Quote, With commercial operations underway and significant demand for our technology, we are focused on delivering the remaining Phase One GeoBlocks on schedule and building toward geothermal as a reliable, always-on, low-cost source of power, end quote.

GeoBlock One, at thirty-three megawatts, is operating in line with expectations for production and power delivered under its power purchase agreement, the company said. GeoBlock Two has achieved grid sync and is in its early operations phase, delivering test energy to the grid. GeoBlock Three is mechanically complete and in cold commissioning. GeoBlocks Two and Three remain on schedule and are expected to achieve commercial operation by January first, twenty twenty-seven, which would complete Cape Station Phase One at about one hundred megawatts from the first three GeoBlocks. Management also said it expects a fifty percent improvement in commissioning speed from GeoBlock One to GeoBlock Three.

The Google agreement is the backlog event of the quarter. It is a fifteen-year, grid-connected, take-or-pay contract for three hundred ninety-six megawatts, with first power expected in mid-twenty twenty-eight. Fervo is also committed to offering Google an option to expand offtake by about six hundred megawatts, which would bring the total partnership to nearly one gigawatt. In connection with the agreement, Fervo progressed construction across Cape Station GeoBlocks Four through Eleven, now referred to as Cape Google Four Hundred, which are expected to provide about four hundred megawatts of power under the Google contract beginning in twenty twenty-eight. The Motley Fool takeaway of the call put the expected total investment for Cape Google Four Hundred at two point two billion dollars through twenty twenty-eight. Installed cost targets cited on the call were five thousand five hundred dollars per kilowatt for that project, with a long-term goal of three thousand dollars per kilowatt. Phase One, the first three GeoBlocks, was cited at seven thousand dollars per kilowatt, including shared infrastructure and capitalized interest. Those cost figures are management targets and reported Phase One costs, not a guarantee of future costs.

With the Google agreement, Fervo now has one thousand fifty-four megawatts of capacity under binding power purchase agreements, up about sixty percent from the second quarter, representing approximately eleven point nine billion dollars in contracted revenue. The early development pipeline rose to fifty-four point seven gigawatts from forty-eight point six gigawatts, and land holdings rose to six hundred seventy thousand acres from six hundred fifty thousand acres. An updated heat-initially-in-place study by DeGolyer and MacNaughton, an independent consulting firm, supported about eight point four gigawatts of capacity potential at Cape Station, nearly double a prior estimate of four point three gigawatts, based on deeper drilling results. The company was also selected for two potential Department of Energy awards totaling about twenty million dollars, including funding for appraisal wells in Idaho and Nevada. Drilling for GeoBlocks Four through Eleven was eighteen percent complete, using four H and P Flex Three rigs.

Safety got a brief but specific mention. The company cited a ten one-hundredths total recordable incident rate, and one million hours worked by the facility construction crew without a recordable incident.

A third cluster went to the Google contract and backlog quality. The contract is take-or-pay and fifteen years, which is strong contract structure if the plant is on time. A fifth cluster went to permitting and policy, including whether anything in the company's growth plans depends on federal permitting legislation sometimes discussed as the BAAJA. Latimer's answer, as reported on the call, was that nothing in the go-forward expectations relies on that legislation, that prior guidance has been based on the company's own expectations for federal permitting timelines, and that if such legislation passed, both geothermal and transmission provisions would be additive, because they could improve the regulatory backdrop and permit timelines. He said Fervo stays keenly focused on it as a potential catalyst, even though the growth plans do not rely on it.

A take-or-pay contract is worth a brief plain-language explanation, because it is central to why the Google backlog number is so large. Under a take-or-pay agreement, the buyer pays for a specified amount of energy whether or not it takes every megawatt-hour. That shifts volume risk toward the offtaker, provided the plant is actually on and delivering within the contract's terms. It does not eliminate construction risk, schedule risk, or reservoir risk on Fervo's side. The eleven point nine billion dollar contracted-revenue figure assumes those plants get built and perform. The three hundred ninety-six megawatt contract, the mid-twenty twenty-eight first-power date, and the two point two billion dollar investment plan are the work that has to happen before that revenue is real.

The company also scheduled an investor open house at Cape Station for Thursday, November nineteenth, twenty twenty-six, with a tour of the site, including the drill rig and power plant. That is a useful reminder of where this story is now. It is a field visit, not an earnings-model quarter. One hundred sixteen thousand dollars of revenue was a rounding error next to two hundred fifty-six point seven million dollars of quarterly capital expenditure, and the fourth quarter is guided to almost twice that spend. The financial story for the next many quarters will be cash, schedule, and cost per kilowatt, not quarterly sales.

On the stock, published reports after the release described a sharp initial bounce, including one account of a more than twenty-six percent surge, followed by a softer pre-market tone as some analysts cut or adjusted price targets. I am not going to treat a single unofficial print as a close. The important point is that investors were reacting to first power and the Google contract, not to the tiny revenue line, and that the reaction was not one-directional once the spending and timeline details were digested.
