Welcome to this earnings brief for Infleqtion, ticker I-N-F-Q. This episode covers the company's third-quarter results for twenty twenty-six. The quarter ended on September thirtieth. Here is the headline. Infleqtion reported third-quarter revenue of nine point one million dollars, up twenty-five percent from a year earlier. The company also said it had delivered a key technical milestone ahead of schedule: thirty entangled logical qubits working together in a single computation on its Scale system, spelled S-q-a-l-e, using only eighty physical qubits.

Hart also gave a brief update on a Department of Commerce transaction announced in the second quarter. He said the company expects to complete the definitive agreement process later this year, and that under the current structure the amount, up to one hundred million dollars, is expected to be recorded as a financing transaction, with final treatment depending on the definitive agreement and the applicable accounting review. Kinsella used the technical milestone to define the quarter. He said that in May he had described the first quarter in one word, momentum, and that in August the word for the second quarter was acceleration. For the third quarter, he said, the word is execution, specifically technical execution. The company had committed to demonstrating thirty logical qubits by the end of twenty twenty-six, and it delivered ahead of schedule. He said, quote, Logical qubits are the keys to the kingdom for useful quantum computing, end quote.

Gokhale explained what that demonstration actually was. He said all thirty logical qubits worked together in a single computation. The company had aimed for fewer than ten physical qubits per logical qubit and ended up with fewer than three. He said that efficiency reflects an A.I.-driven advance that reduced the number of physical gates needed for logical entanglement. Infleqtion's approach, he said, intentionally converts hard-to-identify errors into atom loss, because a missing atom tells the software exactly where a problem occurred.

The signature demonstration, he said, ran an algorithm on thirty logical qubits using about one thousand physical operations, which he called one Kilo-Quop. Circuit-level fidelity after those operations was twenty-one percent, versus a random-guessing success rate of just two hundredths of one percent. When the company error-corrected for atom-loss events, he said, it boosted system yield by a factor of thirty-six while reducing application-level fidelity by a factor of three. The company also prepared thirty logical qubits in what he called a Tesseract error-correction code, with a distance of four, meaning it can be used to correct any single physical error. He said a paper with the technical details would be released within the next few weeks.

The next official milestone is one hundred logical qubits capable of one million reliable operations, or one Mega-Quop, in twenty twenty-eight, and a longer-term roadmap toward one thousand logical qubits in twenty thirty. Gokhale said the company now expects to reach Mega-Quop capability with as few as two thousand physical qubits, a significant improvement in efficiency relative to prior estimates. That system, he said, will integrate continuous atom reloading and repeated error correction so the machine can replenish atoms and keep correcting errors during long computations. Kinsella tied customer deployments and the jump in remaining performance obligations to growing confidence that compute revenue will grow strongly in twenty twenty-seven and will represent a meaningfully larger share of total revenue.

On the commercial computing side, management pointed to several named items. In Japan, the company's quantum processing unit now powers what they described as the country's first operational full-stack neutral atom quantum computer, at the Institute for Molecular Science, under Japan's Quantum Moonshot program. The company already has a deployed system at the United Kingdom's National Quantum Computing Centre. At the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park, Gokhale said the company is finalizing site readiness as it prepares to deliver a quantum processing unit in twenty twenty-seven. In energy, he said a customer, Eaton, is working on grid contingency analysis and outage planning. In finance, he said a leading global financial institution, which he did not name, ran tens of thousands of shots of a thirty-logical-qubit circuit on the Scale system in the third quarter. The company also announced collaborations with NVIDIA, Cisco, and Riverlane.

Sensing was the other half of the business update. The company's Ticker clock, spelled T-i-q-k-e-r, demonstrated sustained operation aboard a U.S. Navy support vessel, the R.V. Merlin, at sea. NASA expanded a quantum gravity contract by twenty million dollars, bringing the cumulative value to forty million dollars, for work with the Jet Propulsion Laboratory that Kinsella said aims to fly the world's first quantum gravity sensor in space, with work expected to continue through twenty twenty-seven. In October, the United Kingdom government awarded an approximately twelve million dollar contract to advance the company's Quantum Spectrum direction-finding technology toward a deployable product. Kinsella said a Quantum Spectrum atom-based antenna can be as small as a sugar cube, versus conventional antenna arrays that can be as big as a football field, and that the system receives entirely passively.

Kinsella closed the prepared remarks by saying that as computing systems begin solving commercially relevant problems and the sensing business grows, the company expects revenue to grow faster than operating costs, which is how it will build operating leverage over time. That is a forward-looking claim, not a result this quarter. This quarter, costs and losses widened by much more than revenue grew.

Now to the question-and-answer session.

Jesse Sobelson of B.T.I.G. asked how Infleqtion's mix might evolve between full-stack system deliveries and cloud or software access. Kinsella said that if you had asked him five years ago, he would have thought that by twenty twenty-six the vast majority of commercial quantum activity would be taking place in the cloud. In reality, he said, it has been the exact opposite, and the market is almost entirely driven by system sales. His current mental model is that system sales will be a big part of the mix for a while, with cloud rising as a percentage over time, and that customers will want machines on their premises early on before shifting more toward cloud consumption. On a follow-up about remaining performance obligations, he said both computing and sensing had contributed, with compute particularly strong.

Craig Ellis of B. Riley Securities asked what the company had learned getting to thirty logical qubits that gives it confidence in the jump to one hundred. Gokhale said getting to thirty validated key building blocks for one hundred as well, notably the ability to pick up qubits and move them around during a computation, which is why the physical-to-logical ratio is so efficient, and the ability to maintain good performance of the underlying physical gates. To get to one hundred logical qubits, he said, the company will need error-correction codes with a greater distance than the distance-four results already shown, and it will need to reload atoms as they are lost. Ellis then asked how customer conversations had evolved. Kinsella said the company had already started to invest more in compute in the third quarter, because conversations had increased globally and become more specific about applications.

Adrienne Colby, asking for Atif Malik at Citi, asked about the timing of the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park delivery and the mix of compute versus sensing in twenty twenty-seven. Kinsella said the plan is to deliver the system to the park by the end of twenty twenty-seven, and that the company will book that revenue in twenty twenty-seven. For modeling purposes, he said, you can book that ten million dollars as revenue next year, and that this will start to take compute up as a percentage of revenue in twenty twenty-seven compared with twenty twenty-six. Hart agreed. On a follow-up about where the company needs to invest, Kinsella pointed to manufacturing, saying Infleqtion needs to be able to manufacture not only sensing products but computers in greater numbers than in the past.

Ella Dolchinsky of Morgan Stanley asked about the comment that customer engagements are shifting to co-development. Gokhale said a lot of recent interest has come from buyers realizing that the cost of running something on a quantum computer can be one hundred or one thousand times lower than their initial estimates, thanks to the reconfigurability of neutral atoms, and that materials, batteries, solar cells, and catalysts are among the earliest expected applications.

John McPeake of Rosenblatt asked whether a forthcoming paper would include a two-qubit gate error rate. Gokhale said the company prefers to measure success at the level of the entire circuit, but that running about one thousand operations at a net fidelity of twenty-one percent implies a per-gate success rate of about ninety-nine point eight five percent, averaged across many types of gates. McPeake asked whether the Mega-Quop machine would support universal gates. Gokhale said yes.

Tyler Anderson of Craig-Hallum, asking for Richard Shannon, wanted to know whether contract manufacturers were a priority for the one-hundred-logical-qubit machine or for a later machine. Kinsella said the company is looking at potential contract manufacturers first for some of its sensing products, and that the timing for computers is still an internal decision. Kingsley Crane of Canaccord Genuity asked whether political crosscurrents and the recent election had changed the timeline of government awards, including the Department of Commerce transaction. Kinsella said no, and that the company was getting closer to the finish line on that deal. Crane also asked about Intel's acquisition of Classiq. Gokhale said Infleqtion's own combination of hardware and software, after the twenty twenty-two acquisition of Super.tech, was being validated by that kind of deal, and that the bulk of the company's software attention is on deep co-design for neutral atoms on Superstat, spelled Superstaq.

Quinn Bolton of Needham and Company closed with a technical exchange worth translating into plain language. He asked whether a factory-level fidelity of ninety-nine point eight five percent implied a logical error rate of roughly ten to the minus three, and whether the Mega-Quop machine would need something like ten to the minus six. Gokhale said that was effectively the right way to think about it, and that getting from ten to the minus three to ten to the minus six is not a one-thousand-times-harder problem, because quantum error correction becomes more powerful as physical qubits are added. Bolton then asked whether the thirty-logical-qubit demonstration involved many live rounds of error correction. Gokhale said those results used a single round, and that the next step toward one hundred logical qubits and one Mega-Quop is to do many rounds. The milestone is real, but it is not yet the multi-round error correction the twenty twenty-eight machine will require.

Here is what is fair to watch next.

First, whether the fourth quarter actually delivers the reversal management described. The maintained full-year outlook implies a fourth-quarter revenue figure around twelve point five million dollars, and management said both revenue and gross margin should improve after a timing-driven third quarter.

Second, conversion of the fifty-one point two million dollars of remaining performance obligations, and whether that balance keeps growing in the fourth quarter the way Kinsella said he expects. Thirty-six point one million dollars of the current R.P.O. is slated for twenty twenty-seven, but R.P.O. is not the same thing as recognized revenue, and a large share of current revenue already comes from a single customer and from government programs.

Third, the computing mix in twenty twenty-seven. Management is asking investors to believe that compute becomes a meaningfully larger share of the business next year, and Kinsella pointed to about ten million dollars of revenue from the Illinois park system as a modeling input, with delivery planned by the end of twenty twenty-seven.

Fourth, the path from a thirty-logical-qubit, single-round demonstration to a one-hundred-logical-qubit, Mega-Quop machine in twenty twenty-eight. Gokhale said key building blocks have been de-risked, and that multiple rounds of error correction, atom reloading, and systems engineering are the work ahead.

Fifth, cash use and operating leverage. The company has five hundred sixty million dollars of cash and securities and no debt, a long runway at the current quarterly operating cash use of sixteen point three million dollars, but that cash figure includes a twenty-eight million dollar temporary payroll-tax benefit, and both operating losses and capital spending are rising. The leverage thesis is that revenue will grow faster than operating costs. This quarter does not yet show it.

Sixth, the Department of Commerce transaction of up to one hundred million dollars. Management still expects a definitive agreement later this year and currently expects to record it as financing, but it is not closed.
