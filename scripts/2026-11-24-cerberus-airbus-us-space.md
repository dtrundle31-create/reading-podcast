Investment firm Cerberus to buy space business of US Airbus arm. This article comes from Breaking Defense, written by Theresa Hitchens, and was published on November 24th, 2026.

Cerberus Capital Management is set to acquire the Space Systems business of Airbus U.S. Space and Defense, the American arm of the European aerospace giant Airbus. The investment firm announced the deal on November 24th, and it is expected to close by the end of the year.

The business being sold makes satellites. It is headquartered in Merritt Island, Florida, and it manufactures them at a facility of about two hundred thousand square feet, sitting next to the Kennedy Space Center. That location places the factory alongside one of the country's main launch sites, a practical detail for a company whose work is building spacecraft.

What the announcement does not include is a price. Breaking Defense notes that Airbus does not break out the annual revenue of its U.S. arm, and that Cerberus did not attach a purchase price to the acquisition. The public picture of the deal, then, is about who is buying what, and why, rather than how large a check is changing hands or how much money the unit already brings in.

Cerberus framed the purchase around customer demand for a particular kind of space architecture, and around a plan to put the business under American ownership. "Customer demand for resilient, proliferated space architectures is driving the need for reliable, scalable U.S. spacecraft production capacity," the firm's announcement said. "Cerberus intends to invest in the Space Systems business's engineering organization, product portfolio, and U.S. supply chain to build a leading independent, U.S.-owned satellite platform."

That language is doing several things at once. Proliferated space architectures, as the term is used here, points to constellations made up of many satellites working together, rather than a small number of very expensive spacecraft. Cerberus is arguing that customers want those architectures to be resilient, and that meeting that demand requires production capacity in the United States that can be counted on and scaled up. The firm's stated answer is to invest in the unit's engineers, in what it builds, and in its American supply chain, and to operate it as an independent, U.S.-owned satellite platform.

That last point is one of the most important in the announcement. The Space Systems business has, until now, sat inside the American subsidiary of a European company. After the sale, if the deal closes as planned, it would no longer be part of Airbus. Cerberus is presenting that change in ownership as a feature of the transaction, not a side effect.

Former Space Force chief Jay Raymond, who is now a senior managing director of Cerberus Supply Chain, tied the purchase to national security. In a statement, he pointed to the growing importance of space to the United States and to allied nations. "With dedicated U.S. ownership and continued investment, we believe the Space Systems unit can innovate and scale to meet growing mission requirements," he said.

Raymond's comment lines up with the firm's own announcement: ownership in the United States, more investment, and a bet that the satellite business can grow to meet rising demand. Breaking Defense identifies Cerberus Supply Chain as a fund established in 2022 and dedicated to supporting defense-related companies. Raymond's move from leading the Space Force to a senior role at that fund is part of how the deal is being presented — as a national-security-minded purchase, not only a financial one.

Airbus, for its part, is describing the sale as a way to concentrate on something else. A spokesperson for Airbus U.S. said in an email that the company "is doubling down on its core aviation business and expanding its autonomous aircraft capabilities, while continuing to pursue new opportunities that drive long-term growth in the U.S. defense market." Breaking Defense reports that Airbus intends for its U.S. subsidiary to double down on production of autonomous aircraft as the market for drones continues to expand.

That is the other half of the story. Airbus is presenting the sale as a shift in emphasis, not a withdrawal from the American defense market. In the company's account, the U.S. subsidiary would put more of its energy into aviation, including autonomous aircraft, which Airbus sees as a growing market, and would keep looking for new work that supports long-term growth with U.S. defense customers. The Space Systems business in Florida is what the company is choosing to sell in order to make that shift.

Alex Veneziano, the chief executive of Airbus U.S. Space and Defense, made a similar case in a statement. "As autonomous systems become increasingly central to the future of defense, this renewed strategic focus will enable Airbus U.S. Space and Defense to strengthen its competitive position, advance innovation, and respond more effectively to the evolving needs of U.S. defense and national security customers," he said.

In other words, Airbus is arguing that a narrower focus — on aviation and on autonomous systems — will help the remaining U.S. business compete, innovate, and respond to what American defense and national security customers want. Veneziano's statement does not linger on the satellite factory being sold. It looks ahead to the work Airbus wants to keep.

Taken together, the two companies are telling complementary stories about the same transaction. Cerberus wants a U.S.-owned satellite manufacturer that it can invest in and scale, at a moment when, in the firm's telling, customers are asking for more resilient, more numerous spacecraft and for production capacity on American soil. Airbus wants to put more of its U.S. effort into aviation and autonomous aircraft, and says that focus will help it serve American defense and national security customers. The Space Systems unit in Merritt Island is the asset that makes both of those arguments possible: a satellite factory next to Kennedy Space Center, now slated to change hands by the end of the year.

What remains unknown, at least from this announcement, is the price of the deal and the financial size of the business being sold. Those figures were not disclosed. The timeline, though, is fairly near-term. Cerberus says the acquisition is set to be completed by the end of the year.
